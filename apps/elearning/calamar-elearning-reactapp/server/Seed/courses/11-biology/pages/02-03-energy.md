# Energy: respiration and photosynthesis

Living things are constantly building, moving and pumping, and all of it costs energy. This page follows energy from sunlight into sugar and from sugar into the cell's universal currency, ATP.

## ATP, the energy currency

**ATP** (adenosine triphosphate) carries energy in the bonds between its three phosphate groups. Splitting off one phosphate releases energy that the cell couples to work: contracting a muscle, pumping ions, building a protein.

$$
\text{ATP} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{ADP} + \text{P}_i, \qquad \Delta G^{\circ\prime} \approx -30.5\ \text{kJ/mol}
$$

$\Delta G$ is the change in free energy; negative means the reaction releases energy and can run on its own. (In real cells, where ATP is kept far above equilibrium, the value is closer to $-50$ kJ/mol.)

A cell doesn't store much ATP: it recycles it. Your body holds only about 50 g at any time, yet it turns over roughly its own body weight in ATP every day.

## Cellular respiration

Respiration burns glucose with oxygen, in many small, controlled steps, and captures the energy as ATP:

$$
\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\,\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 6\,\text{CO}_2 + 6\,\text{H}_2\text{O}, \qquad \Delta G^{\circ\prime} \approx -2870\ \text{kJ/mol}
$$

It runs in three stages:

| Stage | Where | What happens | ATP per glucose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Glycolysis** | cytoplasm | glucose (6 carbons) is split into 2 pyruvate (3 carbons) | 2 |
| **Krebs cycle** (citric acid cycle) | mitochondrial matrix | pyruvate is broken down to CO₂; electrons are loaded onto carriers (NADH, FADH₂) | 2 |
| **Oxidative phosphorylation** | inner mitochondrial membrane | the electrons pass down a chain of proteins to oxygen; the energy pumps protons, whose flow back drives ATP synthase | about 26–28 |

Total: about **30–32 ATP** per glucose (older textbooks say 36–38; better measurements lowered it). How efficient is that?

$$
\text{efficiency} \approx \frac{32 \times 30.5}{2870} \approx 34\,\%
$$

comparable to a good car engine, with the rest released as heat, which keeps you warm.

**ATP synthase**, the enzyme that makes most of your ATP, is a true rotary motor: protons flowing through it turn a rotor about a hundred times per second, and each turn makes three ATP.

### Without oxygen: fermentation

When oxygen is short, cells can run glycolysis alone, for just 2 ATP per glucose. To keep it going they must recycle the electron carrier, so they dump the electrons onto pyruvate:

- in your muscles during a sprint, pyruvate becomes **lactate**;
- in yeast, it becomes **ethanol and CO₂**, which is how bread rises and beer and wine are made.

Fermentation gives about 2/32, or 6 %, of the energy of respiration from the same glucose, which is why it is used only as a backup in our cells.

## Photosynthesis

Plants, algae and cyanobacteria run the reverse reaction, powered by light:

$$
6\,\text{CO}_2 + 6\,\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{light}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\,\text{O}_2
$$

in two stages, in the **chloroplast**:

1. **Light reactions** (in the thylakoid membranes): chlorophyll absorbs light, mostly red and blue (so leaves reflect green). The energy splits water, releasing **oxygen**, and makes ATP and NADPH.
2. **Calvin cycle** (in the stroma): the ATP and NADPH are used to fix CO₂ into sugar. The key enzyme, **RuBisCO**, is probably the most abundant protein on Earth.

The oxygen you breathe comes from the **water**, not from the CO₂. This was shown in the 1940s with water labeled with the isotope oxygen-18.

How efficient is it? Of the sunlight that falls on a field, typical crops store only about 1 % over a growing season, and even the theoretical maximum is only a few percent. Low, but over the whole planet it fixes on the order of 100 billion tonnes of carbon a year and feeds almost every food chain.

## The two processes together

Photosynthesis and respiration form a cycle: plants turn CO₂, water and light into sugar and oxygen; both plants and animals turn sugar and oxygen back into CO₂ and water, capturing the energy as ATP. Energy flows through the cycle once and leaves as heat; the matter goes round and round. The ecology chapter follows the same idea at the scale of ecosystems.
