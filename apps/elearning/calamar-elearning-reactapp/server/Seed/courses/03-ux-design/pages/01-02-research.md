# User research

Research replaces opinions ("users want a dashboard") with evidence ("four of six interviewees export the data to a spreadsheet every Monday").

## Choosing a method

| Method | Tells you | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Interviews** | goals, motivations, workarounds, vocabulary | discovery |
| **Contextual inquiry** (watching people at work) | what people *actually* do, which often differs from what they say | discovery |
| **Surveys** | how common something is | after interviews gave you the right questions |
| **Analytics** | what happens, at scale | anytime; it never tells you *why* |
| **Usability tests** | where a design fails | develop and deliver (chapter 5) |
| **Card sorting** | how people group your content | information architecture |

Qualitative methods (interviews, observation) find *what* and *why*. Quantitative ones (surveys, analytics) find *how many*. Use both.

## Interviewing well

- Ask about **past behavior**, not future intentions: "Tell me about the last time you booked a trip" beats "Would you use a trip planner?"
- Ask open questions, then stay quiet. Silence makes people elaborate.
- Don't pitch or explain your idea. You are there to learn, not to persuade.
- Five to eight interviews per user group usually reveal the main patterns.

## Making research usable

- **Personas**: short, evidence-based portraits of user groups (goals, context, frustrations). They are useful only if they come from research. A persona invented in a meeting just encodes the team's assumptions.
- **Jobs to be done**: frame needs as "When *situation*, I want to *motivation*, so I can *outcome*". For example: "When a client pays late, I want to send a polite reminder in one click, so I can stay friendly and get paid."
- **Journey maps**: the steps a person goes through, with what they do, think and feel at each one. The low points are your opportunities.

## Exercise

Pick an app you use every day. Write three jobs-to-be-done statements for it. Then find one step where the app makes the job harder than it needs to be.
