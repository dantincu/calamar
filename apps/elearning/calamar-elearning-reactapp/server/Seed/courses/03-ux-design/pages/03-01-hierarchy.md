# Visual hierarchy and Gestalt

**Visual hierarchy** is the order in which the eye reads a screen. You control it with:

| Lever | More important looks… |
|---|---|
| Size | larger |
| Weight | bolder |
| Contrast | further from the background (darker on light, lighter on dark) |
| Color | saturated, or the accent color, against neutrals |
| Position | top and left, in left-to-right languages |
| Space | surrounded by more whitespace |

Use as few levers as possible. Making a heading larger *and* bolder *and* colored *and* uppercase is shouting. **One primary action per screen** is the most important application of hierarchy, and color is the strongest tool for it: an accent-colored button among neutral ones.

## Gestalt principles

Gestalt psychology describes how we group what we see:

- **Proximity**: things close together belong together. This is the cheapest grouping tool there is: space a label 4 px from its field and 24 px from the next field.
- **Similarity**: things that look alike (same color, shape or size) seem to have the same function. So **don't give unrelated things the same color**: if links and success messages are both green, people will try to click the messages.
- **Common region**: things inside one border or background belong together (cards).
- **Continuity**: the eye follows lines and alignments.
- **Figure and ground**: we separate an object from its background. Overlays and modals depend on this, and so do dark mode elevation levels (chapter 4).

## The squint test

Squint at your screen, or blur a screenshot, until you can't read any text. What remains is the hierarchy: what stands out first, second and third? Converting the screenshot to grayscale works too, and it also checks that the hierarchy doesn't depend on hue alone.
