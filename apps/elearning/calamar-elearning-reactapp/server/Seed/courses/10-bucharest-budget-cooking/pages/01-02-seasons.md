# Eating with the seasons

Produce in season is grown nearby in the open field, so it is **cheaper, tastier and often more nutritious** than produce grown in greenhouses or transported for a long time. In Bucharest's markets, the price of a vegetable can fall to a third or less between its first weeks (*trufandale*, the first early produce) and the peak of its season.

## The Romanian seasons

| Season | Cheap and good | Expensive (skip or use frozen/canned) |
|---|---|---|
| **Winter** (Dec–Feb) | cabbage, sauerkraut (*varză murată*), potatoes, onions, carrots, beetroot, celeriac, parsnips, stored apples and pears; imported citrus is at its cheapest | tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, zucchini, berries |
| **Spring** (Mar–May) | wild garlic (*leurdă*), nettles (*urzici*), spinach, sorrel (*ștevie*), *lobodă*, lettuce, spring onions, green garlic, radishes; the first strawberries and cherries in May | the first tomatoes and new potatoes (*cartofi noi*) |
| **Summer** (Jun–Aug) | tomatoes, cucumbers, zucchini, green beans, peppers, eggplant, sweetcorn, new cabbage, cherries, apricots, peaches, watermelon, melons | stored winter vegetables are fine, but the old crop is ending |
| **Autumn** (Sep–Nov) | **the best month for the budget**: kapia and *gogoșari* peppers, eggplant (for *zacuscă*), tomatoes in September, cabbage for pickling, potatoes and onions by the sack, pumpkin, apples, pears, plums, grapes, quinces, walnuts | summer fruit |

Some things are cheap all year: dry legumes, cornmeal, oats, rice, flour, eggs, carrots, onions, cabbage and frozen vegetables.

## Spring greens

Nettles, wild garlic, sorrel and *lobodă* appear in the markets in March and April, for a few weeks. They are rich in vitamin C, folate and iron, and they are the traditional end of the winter diet: *ciorbă de urzici*, *mâncare de urzici*, *ciorbă de lobodă*. Buy them fresh (handle nettles with gloves until they are blanched), or blanch and freeze them.

Don't pick wild plants yourself unless you know them well: wild garlic looks like lily of the valley and autumn crocus, which are poisonous.

## Price per kilogram over a season

Prices fall as supply rises. A rough model of a vegetable's price over its season is

$$
p(t) \approx p_{\min} + (p_{\text{start}} - p_{\min})\, e^{-t/\tau}
$$

where $t$ is the number of weeks since the first produce, and $\tau$ is a few weeks. If greenhouse tomatoes cost 18 lei/kg in May and field tomatoes 5 lei/kg in August, waiting saves

$$
1 - \frac{5}{18} \approx 72\,\%
$$

For a family that eats 3 kg of tomatoes a week, that is $3 \times (18 - 5) = 39$ lei a week. In May, canned tomatoes (about 10 lei/kg) make a better sauce for less.

## Preserving the season

The Romanian tradition of preserving (*conserve de casă*) moves summer and autumn produce into winter:

- **Sauerkraut and pickles** (*murături*): cabbage, green tomatoes, cucumbers, cauliflower, in brine. Cheap, rich in vitamin C, and good for the gut, but salty: rinse them and keep portions moderate.
- ***Zacuscă***: roasted eggplant and peppers with onion and tomato. Make it in autumn when eggplant costs about 3 lei/kg.
- **Freezing**: peppers (roasted or raw), tomatoes (whole, for sauces), green beans, peas, berries, plums. A freezer is the best budget tool in the kitchen.
- **Drying**: plums, apples, herbs.

The canning safety rules matter: clean jars, enough acid or salt, and proper heat treatment. Bulging lids or a bad smell mean the jar must be thrown away, without tasting.
