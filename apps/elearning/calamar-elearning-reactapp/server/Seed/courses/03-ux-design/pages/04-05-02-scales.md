# Tonal scales, placed by contrast

A **tonal scale** is one hue in many lightness steps: `brand-50` (almost white) to `brand-950` (almost black). Components use different steps for different jobs: 50 for tinted backgrounds, 200 for borders, 600 for buttons and 700 for text.

## The naive way, and why it fails

"Step 100 is 95% lightness, step 200 is 90%…" in HSL gives uneven steps (lesson 2), and the contrast varies with the hue: an HSL "blue-600" may pass 4.5:1 while "green-600" fails. Then every team member has to check every pair.

## Placing steps by their contrast with white

Instead, give each step a **target contrast with white** $R_s$, and find the color of the hue that hits it:

| Step | 50 | 100 | 200 | 300 | 400 | 500 | 600 | 700 | 800 | 900 | 950 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $R_s$ | 1.05 | 1.15 | 1.35 | 1.7 | 2.4 | 3.3 | 4.6 | 6.2 | 8.5 | 11.5 | 14.5 |

The ratios grow roughly geometrically, by a factor of about 1.3 to 1.4 per step, which feels even. Each target fixes the step's luminance:

$$
Y_s = \frac{1.05}{R_s} - 0.05
$$

Because contrast ratios divide (the *Contrast* lesson), the contrast between any two steps is known in advance, **for every hue**:

$$
\mathrm{CR}(s, t) = \frac{R_t}{R_s} \qquad (s \text{ lighter than } t)
$$

So the step numbers carry guarantees:

| Pair | Contrast | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| 600+ on white | $\ge 4.6$ | body text, button backgrounds with white text |
| 500+ on white | $\ge 3.3$ | icons, input borders, large text |
| 700 on 50 | $6.2 / 1.05 = 5.9$ | text on a tinted background |
| 800 on 100 | $8.5 / 1.15 = 7.4$ | AAA text on a tinted background |
| 50 on 950 (dark mode) | $14.5 / 1.05 = 13.8$ | text in dark mode |

## Chroma

Colors near white and black can't hold much chroma, and pastel tints look better a little desaturated anyway. Start from the base color's $(L_0, C_0)$ and taper the chroma towards both ends:

$$
C(L) = \min\!\left(C_0 \cdot \min\!\left(1,\ \frac{L(1 - L)}{L_0(1 - L_0)}\right),\ C_{\max}(L, h)\right)
$$

$L(1 - L)$ is a parabola that is 0 at black and white and peaks at $L = 0.5$. Normalizing it by its value at $L_0$ keeps full chroma around the base color. $C_{\max}(L, h)$ is the gamut limit from the OKLCH lesson.

## Finding $L$ for each step

The contrast with white falls steadily as $L$ rises, so a bisection on $L \in [0, 1]$ finds the lightness that hits $R_s$ to any precision:

```js
// scale.js
let lo = 0, hi = 1;
for (let i = 0; i < 40; i++) {
  const l = (lo + hi) / 2;
  const cr = contrastRatio(oklchToRgb({ l, c: chromaAt(l), h }), WHITE);
  if (cr < ratio) hi = l; else lo = l;   // not enough contrast: go darker
}
```

## The result for `#4f46e5`

`generateScale('#4f46e5')` returns:

| Step | Hex | OKLCH | CR on white |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | `#f8f9ff` | 98.3% 0.008 277 | 1.05 |
| 100 | `#ebeeff` | 95.3% 0.023 277 | 1.15 |
| 200 | `#d6dcff` | 90.1% 0.048 277 | 1.35 |
| 300 | `#b9c3ff` | 83.1% 0.084 277 | 1.70 |
| 400 | `#94a0ff` | 73.6% 0.137 277 | 2.40 |
| 500 | `#7881ff` | 65.7% 0.184 277 | 3.30 |
| 600 | `#6060fb` | 58.1% 0.224 277 | 4.60 |
| 700 | `#5047e6` | 51.4% 0.230 277 | 6.20 |
| 800 | `#402dcc` | 44.3% 0.227 277 | 8.50 |
| 900 | `#3113ad` | 37.1% 0.215 277 | 11.50 |
| 950 | `#25008b` | 30.6% 0.189 277 | 14.50 |

Notice that **the brand color lands on step 700**: `#5047e6` is almost exactly `#4f46e5`, which has 6.29:1 on white. Brand colors don't have to be step 500. Step numbers describe contrast, and the brand color falls wherever its contrast puts it. If you want the exact brand hex in the palette, replace the closest step with it and check that its contrast still fits between the neighboring steps.

## Steps between steps

Adjacent steps differ by $\Delta E_{\text{OK}} \approx 0.07$ (600 to 700 here), more than three times the 0.02 that is just noticeable. Hover and pressed states can therefore move one step along the scale and still be clearly visible.
