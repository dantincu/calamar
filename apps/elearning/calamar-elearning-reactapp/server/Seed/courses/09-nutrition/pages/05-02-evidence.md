# Nutrition myths and how to read studies

## Why nutrition science is hard

You can't easily give a thousand people a controlled diet for twenty years. So much of nutrition science comes from **observational studies**: researchers follow what people eat and what happens to them. These studies are useful, but they show **association, not causation**. People who eat more vegetables also tend to smoke less, exercise more and earn more; researchers adjust for such factors, but never perfectly (**confounding**).

The **hierarchy of evidence**, from strongest to weakest:

1. **Systematic reviews and meta-analyses** of many studies.
2. **Randomized controlled trials** (RCTs): people are assigned to diets at random.
3. **Cohort studies**: large groups followed over time.
4. **Case-control and cross-sectional studies.**
5. **Animal and cell studies**, mechanisms, and expert opinion.
6. **Anecdotes and testimonials**: "it worked for me".

The strongest conclusions (fiber, vegetables, unsaturated fat instead of saturated, less salt, fewer sugary drinks, no smoking, little alcohol) are supported at several levels.

## Relative vs absolute risk

Headlines love **relative** risk. Suppose eating 50 g of processed meat a day raises the lifetime risk of colorectal cancer by 18 % (the WHO estimate). If the baseline lifetime risk is about 5 %:

$$
\text{new risk} = 5\,\% \times 1.18 = 5.9\,\%, \qquad \text{absolute increase} = 0.9 \text{ percentage points}
$$

That is a real effect across a population. For an individual, "18 % more" and "about 1 in 110 more people" are the same fact, and both are worth knowing.

The **number needed to harm** (or to treat) puts it directly:

$$
\text{NNH} = \frac{1}{\text{absolute risk increase}} = \frac{1}{0.009} \approx 111
$$

## Questions to ask about a claim

1. **What kind of study?** In humans, animals or cells? Observational or randomized?
2. **How big was the effect, in absolute terms?**
3. **How many people, and for how long?** A two-week study of 20 people is a hint, not a conclusion.
4. **Compared with what?** "Eggs are healthy" compared with a sausage, or with oats?
5. **Who paid for it, and who is telling you?** Is someone selling a product, a book or a supplement?
6. **Does it agree with the rest of the evidence?** One study that overturns decades of research is usually wrong.

## Common myths

| Claim | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| "Carbs make you fat." | Excess energy makes you fat. Low-carb and low-fat diets give similar weight loss. Fiber-rich carbohydrates are linked to better health. |
| "Eating late makes you fat." | Total intake matters most. Late snacking often *adds* calories, which is the real problem. |
| "Detox" juices and teas | The liver and kidneys detoxify the body. No product "cleanses" it. |
| "Eggs raise cholesterol." | For most people, dietary cholesterol has a small effect on blood cholesterol; saturated fat has a larger one. An egg a day is fine for most healthy people. |
| "Brown sugar and honey are healthier." | They are sugar: free sugars, with traces of minerals. |
| "Gluten-free is healthier." | Only for people with coeliac disease or gluten sensitivity. Gluten-free products are often lower in fiber. |
| "Fresh is always better than frozen." | Frozen vegetables are frozen hours after harvest, and keep their vitamins well. |
| "You need 8 glasses of water." | Total water needs include food; thirst is a good guide for most adults. |
| "Superfoods" | No single food makes a diet healthy. Cabbage, beans and oats are as "super" as goji berries, at a fraction of the price. |
| "Metabolism-boosting" foods | Effects of chili, green tea or coffee on energy use are tiny, a few kcal a day. |

## Reliable sources

- **EFSA** (European Food Safety Authority) and the **WHO**, for reference values and guidelines.
- The **Romanian Ministry of Health** and the **Institutul Național de Sănătate Publică** (INSP) for national guidance.
- Registered dietitians: in Romania, members of the *Colegiul Dieteticienilor din România*.
- Be wary of anyone who sells the solution they recommend.
