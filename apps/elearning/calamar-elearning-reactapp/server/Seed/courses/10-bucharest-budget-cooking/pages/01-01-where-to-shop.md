# Markets and supermarkets

## The markets (*piețe agroalimentare*)

Bucharest has dozens of markets run by the sector administrations. For seasonal vegetables and fruit, eggs, cheese and pickles, they are often the cheapest and best option, especially in summer and autumn.

| Market | Where | Known for |
|---|---|---|
| **Piața Obor** | Sector 2, Obor metro (M1) | the largest in the city; usually among the lowest prices; a hall for cheese and meat, sacks of potatoes and onions in autumn |
| **Piața Rahova** | Sector 5 | large and inexpensive |
| **Piața Crângași** | Sector 6, Crângași metro | a busy neighborhood market |
| **Piața Moghioru** | Sector 6, Drumul Taberei | large; can be a little more expensive than Obor |
| **Piața Ilioara** | Sector 3, Titan | the market of a big residential area |
| **Piața Progresul** | Sector 4 | large, inexpensive |
| **Piața Amzei, Dorobanți, Floreasca** | Sector 1 | central and northern; good quality, usually **more expensive** |

Prices differ between markets, and between stalls in the same market, often by 30 % or more for the same vegetable. Walk the whole row before you buy.

### Tips for the market

- **Producers vs resellers.** Some sellers grow what they sell (*producători*, who must hold an *atestat de producător*); many are resellers (*intermediari*) who buy from wholesalers. Both can be good. Romanian produce in season is usually cheaper and tastier than imported produce out of season.
- **Go early for choice, late for prices.** Early morning has the best selection. In the last hour, and on Sunday afternoon, some sellers lower prices rather than take produce home.
- **Buy in quantity in autumn.** In September and October, potatoes, onions, cabbage and peppers are sold by the sack or the box, much cheaper per kilogram. Only buy what you can store (next chapter).
- **Weigh it yourself.** Check the price per kg on the label, and how much the bag weighs.
- **Bring bags and small change.** Many stalls take cards now, but not all.
- **Negotiating** is normal for large amounts; for one kilogram, less so.
- **Cheese and dairy**: buy telemea and *brânză de vaci* in the dairy hall (*hala de lactate*), and taste before buying. Ask how salty it is.

## The supermarkets

| Chain | Typical position |
|---|---|
| **Lidl**, **Penny**, **Kaufland** | discount and low prices; the best for dry staples, dairy, frozen food |
| **Profi** | many small shops in residential areas; prices close to the discounters |
| **Carrefour**, **Auchan** | hypermarkets with a large range; own brands are cheap |
| **Mega Image** | everywhere in the center, open late; convenient but usually more expensive |
| **Selgros**, **Metro** | cash and carry for businesses (a card is needed); good for bulk |

How to pay less at the supermarket:

- **Own brands** (the store's brand) are usually 20–40 % cheaper than branded products, and for staples such as oats, lentils, flour, canned tomatoes and frozen vegetables they are often made in the same factories.
- **Loyalty apps** (Lidl Plus, the Kaufland card, the Mega Image and Carrefour apps) give real discounts on staples, typically 15–30 % on a few products a week. The discounts in this course's price list are *not* included, so the apps take your costs below the course's.
- **Weekly leaflets** (*catalog*, *oferte*): plan the week's menu around what's on offer, especially meat, fish and cheese, and stock up on dry staples when they are discounted.
- **Reduced-price shelves** near the expiry date: fine for food you cook or freeze the same day.
- **Look at the bottom shelves**: the cheapest products are rarely at eye level.

## A shopping routine

1. **Saturday**: plan the week's menu (the final chapter shows how), with a list, by section.
2. **Market** for vegetables, fruit, eggs and cheese.
3. **Discounter** for dry staples, dairy, oil, frozen food, canned fish.
4. **Sunday**: batch cook (chapter 3).
5. **Midweek**: a short top-up trip for bread and fresh produce.

Never shop hungry, and buy only what's on the list, apart from good offers on staples you already use.
