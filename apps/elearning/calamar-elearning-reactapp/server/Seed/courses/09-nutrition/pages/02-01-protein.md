# Protein

Proteins are chains of **amino acids**. The body uses them to build and repair muscle and every other tissue, and to make enzymes, hormones and antibodies. It can't store protein the way it stores fat, so you need some every day.

## How much

EFSA's population reference intake for adults is

$$
\text{protein} = 0.83\ \text{g} \times W \quad (\text{body weight in kg})
$$

That covers the needs of 97.5 % of healthy adults: about 54 g/day at 65 kg, and 66 g at 80 kg. Most people in Europe already eat more than that. Some groups benefit from more:

| Who | g per kg per day |
|---|---|
| healthy adults | 0.83 |
| adults over 65 (to slow muscle loss) | 1.0–1.2 |
| strength or endurance training | 1.2–2.0 |
| while losing weight (to keep muscle) | 1.2–1.6 |

Intakes up to about 2 g/kg are considered safe for healthy people. People with kidney disease must follow their doctor's advice instead.

Spreading protein over the day, about 20–40 g per meal, appears to help muscle building more than one large portion.

## Quality: the essential amino acids

Nine amino acids are **essential**: the body can't make them. Animal proteins (eggs, milk, meat, fish) contain all nine in good proportions. Most plant proteins are low in one: cereals in **lysine**, legumes in **methionine**. Eaten together, they make up for each other:

- beans or lentils with bread, rice or mămăligă,
- hummus with bread,
- a bean soup (*ciorbă de fasole*) with bread.

They don't need to be in the same meal; over the day is enough.

## Protein density

To get enough protein without too much energy, compare foods by **protein density**:

$$
\rho_P = \frac{\text{protein (g)}}{\text{energy (kcal)}} \times 100 \quad [\text{g per 100 kcal}]
$$

| Food (per 100 g) | Protein | kcal | g per 100 kcal |
|---|---|---|---|
| chicken breast, raw | 22.5 | 120 | 18.8 |
| cottage cheese (*brânză de vaci*) | 11.1 | 98 | 11.3 |
| eggs | 12.6 | 143 | 8.8 |
| dry lentils | 24.6 | 352 | 7.0 |
| dry white beans | 23.4 | 333 | 7.0 |
| telemea | 14.2 | 264 | 5.4 |
| oats | 13.2 | 379 | 3.5 |
| white bread | 9.0 | 265 | 3.4 |
| walnuts | 15.2 | 654 | 2.3 |

Lentils and beans are among the cheapest sources of protein there are, and they bring fiber with it.

## Quick check

A 70 kg adult eats 3 eggs (165 g), 100 g of dry lentils and 150 g of bread. Is that enough protein?

$$
165 \times 0.126 + 100 \times 0.246 + 150 \times 0.09 = 20.8 + 24.6 + 13.5 = 58.9\ \text{g} \geq 0.83 \times 70 = 58.1\ \text{g}
$$

Yes, just, before counting anything else eaten that day.
