# Plant-based eating and fasting periods

## Vegetarian and vegan diets

Well-planned vegetarian and vegan diets are healthy for adults, and they are linked to lower rates of heart disease and type 2 diabetes. "Well-planned" is the key part: removing animal foods removes the main sources of a few nutrients, which then need attention.

| Nutrient | Vegetarian (eats eggs and dairy) | Vegan |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | usually fine | eat legumes every day; combine with grains |
| Vitamin B12 | eggs and dairy help, but check | **a supplement is essential** |
| Iron | plant iron + vitamin C | plant iron + vitamin C |
| Calcium | dairy | fortified plant drinks, tofu set with calcium, sesame (*tahini*), green vegetables |
| Iodine | dairy, iodized salt | iodized salt |
| Omega-3 (EPA, DHA) | eggs give a little | walnuts and flaxseed (ALA); consider an algae supplement |
| Vitamin D | as for everyone | as for everyone |

B12 deficiency develops slowly, over years, because the liver stores it, and it can damage nerves permanently. For vegans, the common advice is a daily supplement of at least 25–100 µg, or about 2000 µg once a week. Fortified foods help, but are rarely enough alone.

## The Orthodox fasts (*post*)

The Romanian Orthodox calendar has four long fasting periods (Easter, Christmas, the Dormition in August, and the Apostles in June) plus most Wednesdays and Fridays, about 180–200 days a year for those who keep them all. Fasting here means a **vegan diet**: no meat, eggs or dairy. Fish, wine and oil are allowed on some days; the rules vary with the day and the person.

Traditional *mâncare de post* is a healthy, cheap cuisine:

- **legumes**: *fasole bătută* (bean dip), *iahnie de fasole*, lentil stew, bean soup,
- **vegetables**: *zacuscă*, *ghiveci*, *varză călită*, peppers stuffed with rice and vegetables, *sarmale de post*, *salată de vinete*,
- **grains**: mămăligă, rice pilaf with vegetables or mushrooms,
- **nuts and seeds**: walnuts, sunflower seeds, *halva* (energy-dense: eat little).

The usual traps during the fast:

- **Too little protein**, when the fast becomes bread, potatoes and pasta. Eat a portion of legumes, or of fish where allowed, every day.
- **Too much sugar and oil**: *de post* biscuits, *halva*, fried potatoes. "Fasting" food is not automatically healthy; read the label.
- **Vitamin B12** isn't a worry for a few weeks, thanks to the body's stores, but someone who fasts most of the year is close to vegan, and should consider a supplement.

## Protein on a plant-based day

A 65 kg adult needs about 54 g of protein. A fasting day can reach it easily:

| Meal | Food | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| breakfast | 60 g oats with water and fruit, 15 g walnuts | 10 g |
| lunch | lentil stew: 80 g dry lentils, vegetables; 100 g wholemeal bread | 33 g |
| snack | 30 g sunflower seeds | 6 g |
| dinner | *fasole bătută* (60 g dry beans) with vegetables and 60 g bread | 22 g |
| | **total** | **≈ 71 g** |

Check one line: $80 \times 0.246 + 100 \times 0.13 = 19.7 + 13 \approx 33$ g.

People with medical conditions, pregnant women and children shouldn't fast strictly without advice, and priests commonly allow exceptions for the ill, the elderly and pregnant women.
