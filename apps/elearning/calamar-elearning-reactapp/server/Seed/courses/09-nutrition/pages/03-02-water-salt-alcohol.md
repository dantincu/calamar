# Water, salt and alcohol

## Water

EFSA's adequate intake of **total** water, from drinks and food together:

$$
2.0\ \text{L/day (women)}, \qquad 2.5\ \text{L/day (men)}
$$

About 20 % usually comes from food (vegetables, fruit, soups), so drinks provide around 1.6 L and 2.0 L. Needs rise in heat, with exercise, and with fever.

You don't need to count glasses. Drink when thirsty, drink with every meal, and check the color of your urine: pale yellow is right. Older people feel thirst less, so they should drink regularly anyway.

Tap water in Bucharest is treated and monitored, and safe to drink. If old pipes in your building give it a taste, a jug filter helps. Coffee and tea count toward your water intake, though in large amounts caffeine can disturb sleep: EFSA considers up to 400 mg of caffeine a day (about 4 cups of filter coffee) safe for adults, and 200 mg in pregnancy.

## Salt

Salt is sodium chloride. Sodium raises blood pressure, the leading risk factor for strokes and a major one for heart attacks. The WHO guideline:

$$
\text{salt} < 5\ \text{g/day} \quad (\text{about one teaspoon}), \qquad \text{salt} = 2.5 \times \text{sodium}
$$

Most adults in Europe eat around 8–11 g a day, and surveys in Romania have found intakes in that range or higher. Most of it doesn't come from the salt shaker, but from:

- **bread** (about 1–1.2 g per 100 g; 200 g of bread is half the day's limit),
- **cheese**, especially brined cheeses: telemea has about 2.5–3.5 g per 100 g,
- **cured meats**: salami, *parizer*, sausages, *mici*,
- **pickles** (*murături*) and sauerkraut, stock cubes, soup mixes and ready meals.

How to cut down:

- Taste before you salt; cook with herbs (*leuștean* in soups, dill, parsley, garlic), lemon and *borș*, and add salt at the end.
- Soak telemea in water or milk for an hour to remove some salt.
- Choose bread and stock with less salt, and read the labels (next chapter).
- Taste buds adapt: after 2–3 weeks of less salt, food tastes normal again.

## Alcohol

Alcohol has 7 kcal per gram and no nutrients. A standard drink is about 10 g of alcohol:

$$
\text{alcohol (g)} = \text{volume (ml)} \times \frac{\% \text{ABV}}{100} \times 0.789
$$

A 500 ml beer at 5 %: $500 \times 0.05 \times 0.789 \approx 20$ g, two standard drinks and about 140 kcal from the alcohol alone (about 210 kcal with the carbohydrate). A 50 ml *țuică* or *pălincă* at 40 %: $50 \times 0.40 \times 0.789 \approx 16$ g.

Alcohol causes at least seven types of cancer, starting from low intakes, and in 2023 the WHO stated that **no level of alcohol is safe** for health. Earlier claims that moderate drinking protects the heart are now thought to be largely an artifact of how the studies were done (non-drinkers included former drinkers who had quit because they were ill). If you drink, less is better, and having several alcohol-free days a week helps.
