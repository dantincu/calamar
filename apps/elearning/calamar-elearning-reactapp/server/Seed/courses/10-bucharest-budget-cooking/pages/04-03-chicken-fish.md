# Chicken and fish

Meat and fish are the most expensive part of most shopping baskets. Budget cooking doesn't remove them; it uses the cheaper cuts, in smaller portions, with plenty of vegetables.

## Varză călită cu pulpe de pui (braised cabbage with chicken legs)

**4 portions · ≈ 4.34 lei per portion · 458 kcal, 30 g protein, 8 g fiber, 289 g of vegetables**

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| white cabbage | 1.2 kg (1 medium head) |
| chicken legs (*pulpe întregi*) | 800 g (4 small, or 2 large cut in two) |
| onion | 150 g |
| tomato paste | 70 g |
| sunflower oil | 20 g |
| dill | 10 g |
| salt | 4 g |
| paprika, thyme, 2 bay leaves, peppercorns | |

1. Season the chicken with paprika and a little salt. Brown it skin-side down in a dry, heavy pan for 8–10 minutes: the skin releases its own fat. Take the chicken out, and pour off most of the fat.
2. In the same pan, soften the onion in the oil. Add the finely shredded cabbage, a pinch of salt, the thyme and the bay leaves. Stir until it wilts, about 10 minutes.
3. Stir in the tomato paste and a glass of water. Put the chicken on top of the cabbage.
4. Cover and cook in the oven at 180 °C for 50–60 minutes, or on low heat on the stove, until the cabbage is soft and the chicken is cooked through (no pink at the bone).
5. Add the dill at the end.

In winter, use half sauerkraut (rinsed) and half fresh cabbage, and leave out most of the salt: sauerkraut is already salty.

**Why chicken legs?** At about 15 lei/kg they cost half as much as breast, and they stay juicy in long cooking. Their bones are about a quarter of the weight, which `pantry-bucharest.json` accounts for with an edible fraction of 0.75. The skin adds saturated fat: remove it before eating to save about 5 g of fat per leg.

## Macrou la cuptor cu cartofi (baked mackerel with potatoes)

**4 portions · ≈ 6.21 lei per portion · 557 kcal, 33 g protein**

Mackerel is one of the cheapest oily fish: frozen whole mackerel costs about 20 lei/kg at the discounters. One portion gives several days' worth of omega-3 fats (EPA and DHA), and about a day's worth of vitamin D.

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| whole mackerel, thawed | 1 kg (3–4 fish) |
| potatoes | 1 kg |
| onion | 150 g |
| garlic | 3 cloves |
| sunflower oil | 30 g |
| parsley, dill | 15 g |
| salt | 4 g |
| paprika, thyme, lemon or vinegar | |

1. Heat the oven to 200 °C. Cut the potatoes into thick slices, toss with half the oil, paprika and a little salt, and bake for 20 minutes.
2. Clean the fish if needed, rinse inside, pat dry, and make 3 cuts on each side. Rub with garlic, thyme, the rest of the oil and a little salt; put onion slices and parsley inside.
3. Lay the fish on the potatoes and bake 20–25 minutes, until the flesh comes away from the bone.
4. Serve with lemon, and a cabbage or beetroot salad.

The traditional garlic sauce (*mujdei*: crushed garlic with water, oil and salt) goes well with it; make it with less salt.

## Canned fish

A 125 g can of sardines or mackerel costs about 4–6 lei, needs no cooking, and gives 20 g of protein and omega-3. Mash it with a fork, onion, a little lemon and parsley, and spread it on wholemeal bread. Choose fish in water, tomato sauce or olive oil, and eat the soft bones of sardines: they are a good source of calcium.

## Using the whole chicken

A whole chicken (*pui întreg*) is often cheaper per kilogram than any cut:

- legs and thighs for *varză călită*, or a *tocăniță*,
- breasts for a quick dinner, or minced for *perișoare*,
- wings, back and carcass for **stock**, with onion, carrot, parsnip and celery: the base of *supă de pui* and *ciorbe* for a week.

Cutting up a chicken takes ten minutes once you've learnt it.
