# Storing food and avoiding waste

A European household throws away a significant share of the food it buys. Every kilogram thrown away is money spent for nothing, so storage is part of budgeting.

## Where things keep

| Food | Where | How long |
|---|---|---|
| potatoes, onions | cool, dark, dry, **apart** from each other (onions make potatoes sprout) | weeks to months |
| cabbage, carrots, beetroot | fridge drawer, or a cold balcony in winter above 0 °C | 2–4 weeks; cabbage longer |
| apples | fridge, or cool and dark | weeks |
| tomatoes, bananas | room temperature until ripe | days |
| leafy greens, herbs | fridge, wrapped in a damp towel; herbs in a glass of water | 3–7 days |
| eggs | fridge, in their box | up to the date on the box |
| dry legumes, grains, flour | closed jars, dry | months (old beans take longer to cook) |
| cooked food | fridge, ≤ 5 °C, covered | 3–4 days |
| cooked food, bread, meat, fish | freezer, −18 °C | 2–3 months for best quality |

A balcony in a Bucharest winter is a good cold store above freezing, but temperatures swing from below −10 °C to above 10 °C, so check it.

## Food safety

- **Cool cooked food quickly** and get it into the fridge within two hours. Divide a big pot into shallow containers so it cools faster.
- **Reheat until steaming hot** throughout (at least 75 °C in the center), and reheat only once.
- **Rice** can carry the spores of *Bacillus cereus*, which survive cooking. Cool cooked rice quickly and eat it within a day.
- **Dry beans** contain a toxic lectin that only boiling destroys: boil them hard for at least 10 minutes. Never cook dry beans in a slow cooker from raw, and don't eat them undercooked.
- **Thaw meat and fish in the fridge**, not on the counter. Don't refreeze raw meat once it has thawed; you can freeze it again once cooked.
- **Raw chicken**: don't wash it (the splashes spread bacteria), use a separate board, and cook it through until the juices run clear.

## The "use it up" habits

- **First in, first out**: put new shopping at the back of the fridge, and older food at the front.
- **Plan one "leftovers" meal a week**: the last of the vegetables go into a soup (*ciorbă de legume*), an omelette or a *tocăniță*.
- **Stale bread** becomes croutons for soup, breadcrumbs, or *pâine prăjită* with egg.
- **Vegetable trimmings** (carrot peel, onion skins, celery leaves, parsley stalks): keep them in a bag in the freezer, and boil them for stock.
- **Chicken bones**: after a meal of chicken legs, boil the bones with vegetables for a stock.
- **Overripe fruit**: freeze it for porridge, or bake it.
- **Dates on the label**: *a se consuma până la* (use by) is about **safety**; don't eat after it. *A se consuma, de preferință, înainte de* (best before) is about **quality**: dry food is usually fine long after it.

## The cost of waste

If a fraction $w$ of the food you buy is wasted, the real cost of what you eat is

$$
\text{cost per kg eaten} = \frac{\text{cost per kg bought}}{1 - w}
$$

Wasting 20 % makes everything 25 % more expensive: $1 / (1 - 0.2) = 1.25$. Halving the waste is often worth more than any special offer.
