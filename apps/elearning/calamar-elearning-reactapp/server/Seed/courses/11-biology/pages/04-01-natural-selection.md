# Natural selection and its evidence

In 1858 Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace jointly presented the theory of evolution by natural selection, and in 1859 Darwin published *On the Origin of Species*.

## The argument

Natural selection follows from four observations:

1. **Variation**: individuals in a population differ from each other.
2. **Heredity**: some of those differences are inherited.
3. **Overproduction**: more offspring are born than can survive and reproduce.
4. **Differential success**: individuals with some inherited traits survive and reproduce better than others in their environment.

So, over generations, the advantageous inherited traits become more common. Nothing in this argument requires a goal or a plan: selection only keeps what works *now*, in *this* environment.

Darwin didn't know where the variation came from or how it was inherited. The **modern synthesis** of the 1930s and 1940s combined his theory with Mendel's genetics: variation comes from **mutation** and from the reshuffling of genes in sexual reproduction, and evolution is the change of **allele frequencies** in populations, the subject of the next page.

## The mechanisms of evolution

Natural selection is the only mechanism that produces **adaptation**, but not the only one that changes populations:

| Mechanism | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Mutation** | creates new alleles; slow, random with respect to need |
| **Natural selection** | increases alleles that improve survival and reproduction |
| **Genetic drift** | random changes in allele frequencies, strongest in small populations |
| **Gene flow** | migration moves alleles between populations, making them more alike |
| **Sexual selection** | traits that win mates spread, even at a cost to survival (the peacock's tail) |

## The evidence

**Fossils.** The fossil record shows life changing over time, in the order predicted: fish before amphibians, amphibians before reptiles, and transitional forms such as *Tiktaalik*, a fish with limb-like fins, found in 2004 in 375-million-year-old rocks where its discoverers had predicted it would be.

**Comparative anatomy.** The arm of a human, the flipper of a whale, the wing of a bat and the leg of a cat have the same bones in the same arrangement, built for very different jobs: **homologous structures**, inherited from a common ancestor. Vestigial structures, such as the remnants of hip bones in whales, are leftovers of ancestral forms.

**Biogeography.** Islands have species related to those of the nearest mainland, not to those of similar islands elsewhere. The Galápagos finches resemble South American finches; Darwin noticed this pattern.

**Molecular biology.** All life shares the same genetic code, and DNA sequences reveal a family tree that matches the one built from anatomy. Humans and chimpanzees differ by only about 1.2 % of aligned DNA bases. We even share broken genes: the gene for making vitamin C is disabled by the same kind of damage in humans and other apes, a mutation inherited from a common ancestor.

**Evolution observed.** Evolution is happening now:

- **Antibiotic resistance**: bacteria with resistance mutations survive treatment and take over. It is one of the major medical problems of this century.
- **Finches**: Peter and Rosemary Grant measured beak sizes on the Galápagos island Daphne Major for decades. After the 1977 drought, when only large, hard seeds were left, the average beak depth grew measurably in a single generation.
- **The long-term evolution experiment**: since 1988, Richard Lenski's group has followed twelve populations of *E. coli*, for more than 75,000 generations. Around generation 31,000, one population evolved the ability to eat citrate in the presence of oxygen, something *E. coli* normally can't do.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Evolution is just a theory."** In science, a *theory* is a well-tested explanation that unifies many facts, like the theory of gravity. That species evolve is an observed fact; natural selection is the tested theory of how adaptation happens.
- **"Individuals evolve."** Individuals don't evolve; populations do, over generations.
- **"Evolution aims at perfection."** Selection can only work with existing variation, and improves things only relative to the current environment. Our eye has a blind spot because its nerve fibers run in front of the retina; the octopus eye, which evolved separately, doesn't.
- **"Humans descend from chimpanzees."** Humans and chimpanzees share a common ancestor that lived roughly 6–7 million years ago; both lineages have evolved since.
