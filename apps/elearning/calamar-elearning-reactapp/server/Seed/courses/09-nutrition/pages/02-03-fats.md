# Fats

Fat is not the enemy. You need it to absorb vitamins A, D, E and K, to build cell membranes and hormones, and for two fatty acids the body can't make. What matters is the **kind** of fat.

## The kinds of fat

Fats are mostly triglycerides: three fatty acids on a glycerol backbone. Fatty acids differ in their number of double bonds:

| Kind | Double bonds | Found in | Effect on LDL cholesterol |
|---|---|---|---|
| saturated | 0 | butter, cheese, fatty meat, lard, coconut and palm oil | raises |
| monounsaturated | 1 | olive oil, high-oleic sunflower oil, rapeseed oil, nuts | lowers (when replacing saturated) |
| polyunsaturated, omega-6 | 2+ | sunflower oil, walnuts, seeds | lowers (when replacing saturated) |
| polyunsaturated, omega-3 | 3+ | oily fish (mackerel, sardines, herring), walnuts, flaxseed, rapeseed oil | lowers triglycerides |
| trans (industrial) | 1+, "trans" shape | partially hydrogenated oils | raises LDL **and** lowers HDL |

The best-supported advice from decades of trials: **replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fat lowers the risk of heart disease**. Replacing it with refined carbohydrates (white bread, sugar) does not help.

## The targets

$$
\text{total fat: } 20\text{–}35\,\%\ \text{of energy}, \qquad \text{saturated} < 10\,\%, \qquad \text{trans: as low as possible}
$$

At 2000 kcal, 10 % is $200 / 9 \approx 22$ g of saturated fat. 100 g of telemea already has about 15 g.

Since 2021 the EU limits industrial trans fat to 2 g per 100 g of fat in foods sold to consumers, so it is now a small problem in Europe. Small amounts occur naturally in milk and meat.

## Omega-3

The long-chain omega-3 fats **EPA and DHA** are linked to heart health, and DHA is important for the brain and eyes. EFSA advises **250 mg a day** of EPA plus DHA, which **1–2 portions of oily fish a week** provide. Canned mackerel and sardines are as good as fresh, and much cheaper.

Walnuts and flaxseed have a plant omega-3 (ALA), but the body converts only a small fraction of it, typically under 10 %, to EPA and DHA. Vegans can take an algae-based supplement.

## Cooking with fat

- **Sunflower oil**, the default in Romanian kitchens, is rich in omega-6 polyunsaturated fat. That is fine for cooking at normal temperatures. For frying, high-oleic sunflower oil (*cu conținut ridicat de acid oleic*) or olive oil are more stable.
- Don't reuse frying oil many times: it oxidizes.
- Measure oil with a spoon instead of pouring it: at 9 kcal/g, the difference between "a drizzle" and "a pour" can be 200 kcal.

## Quick check

A meal has 30 g of fat, 12 g of it saturated, and 800 kcal in total. Is the saturated fat under 10 % of energy?

$$
\frac{12 \times 9}{800} = \frac{108}{800} = 13.5\,\% \quad \text{(no)}
$$
