# HSL, and why its lightness lies

HSL (hue, saturation, lightness) rearranges the RGB cube into a cylinder that people can reason about: pick a hue on a wheel, then adjust its saturation and lightness. It's in CSS as `hsl(220 64% 47%)`, and in every color picker.

## From RGB to HSL

With channels in $[0, 1]$, let $M = \max(R', G', B')$, $m = \min(R', G', B')$ and $d = M - m$ (the "chroma" of the RGB cube). Then:

$$
L = \frac{M + m}{2}, \qquad
S = \begin{cases} 0 & d = 0 \\[1ex] \dfrac{d}{1 - |2L - 1|} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
$$

$$
H = 60^\circ \times \begin{cases}
\left(\dfrac{G' - B'}{d}\right) \bmod 6 & M = R' \\[2ex]
\dfrac{B' - R'}{d} + 2 & M = G' \\[2ex]
\dfrac{R' - G'}{d} + 4 & M = B'
\end{cases}
$$

For `#2b59c3`: $M = 0.765$, $m = 0.169$, $d = 0.596$, so $L = 0.467$ and $S = 0.596 / (1 - |0.933 - 1|) = 0.639$. Blue is the largest channel, so $H = 60^\circ \times \left(\frac{0.169 - 0.349}{0.596} + 4\right) \approx 222^\circ$.

## From HSL to RGB

CSS Color 4 gives a compact form. With $a = S \cdot \min(L,\ 1 - L)$:

$$
f(n) = L - a \cdot \max\!\big(-1,\ \min(k - 3,\ 9 - k,\ 1)\big), \qquad k = \left(n + \frac{H}{30^\circ}\right) \bmod 12
$$

$$
(R', G', B') = \big(f(0),\ f(8),\ f(4)\big)
$$

## The problem

HSL's $L$ is the midpoint of the largest and smallest channels. It knows nothing about how bright the eye finds each channel. Green looks far brighter than blue at the same channel value (lesson 4 weights them $0.7152$ and $0.0722$). So, at the same HSL lightness:

| Color | HSL | Luminance $Y$ | Contrast with white |
|---|---|---|---|
| yellow `#ffff00` | `hsl(60 100% 50%)` | 0.928 | **1.07 : 1** (invisible) |
| blue `#0000ff` | `hsl(240 100% 50%)` | 0.072 | **8.59 : 1** (fine for text) |

Two colors with the same "lightness" sit at opposite ends of the contrast range. The same happens at any lightness:

| HSL lightness | perceived lightness (OKLab $L$), hue 60° | hue 230° |
|---|---|---|
| 30% | 0.61 | 0.33 |
| 50% | 0.89 | 0.47 |
| 70% | 0.93 | 0.66 |

(All with saturation 80%.)

**Consequences:**

- A scale made by stepping HSL lightness ("blue-100 = L 90%, blue-200 = L 80%…") produces steps that look uneven, and different hues end up with different contrast at the same step number.
- Rotating the hue at a fixed $S$ and $L$ (for color harmonies) changes the perceived lightness drastically.

HSL is fine for picking a color by eye. It is the wrong tool for *computing* a palette. The next lesson introduces the right one.

## In code

```js
import { hslToRgb, luminance, parseHex, rgbToHsl, toHex } from './color.js';

rgbToHsl(parseHex('#2b59c3'));                    // { h: 221.8, s: 0.639, l: 0.467 }
luminance(hslToRgb({ h: 60, s: 1, l: 0.5 }));     // 0.928
luminance(hslToRgb({ h: 240, s: 1, l: 0.5 }));    // 0.072
```
