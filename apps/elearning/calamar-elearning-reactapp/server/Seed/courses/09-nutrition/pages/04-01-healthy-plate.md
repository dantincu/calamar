# The healthy plate

## The plate model

A simple picture covers most of the targets of the previous chapters. At a main meal, fill your plate like this:

| Part of the plate | What | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **½** | vegetables (and fruit) | salads, cabbage, roasted peppers, carrots, beetroot, green beans, a soup full of vegetables |
| **¼** | whole grains and starchy foods | wholemeal bread, mămăligă, potatoes, rice, pasta, oats |
| **¼** | protein foods | beans, lentils, chickpeas, eggs, fish, chicken, cottage cheese |
| a little | healthy fat | a spoon of oil, a handful of nuts or seeds |

Drink water, and have fruit or yogurt as a dessert.

## Portions without a scale

| Food | Portion | Hand measure |
|---|---|---|
| vegetables | 80 g | a fist |
| fruit | 80–150 g | one apple or a fist |
| cooked rice, pasta, mămăligă | 150–180 g | about two fists |
| meat, fish | 100–120 g | the palm of your hand |
| cooked legumes | 150 g | a fist and a half |
| cheese | 30 g | two thumbs |
| nuts | 25–30 g | a small handful |
| oil | 10 g | one tablespoon |

## Fruit and vegetables: at least 400 g

The WHO advises **at least 400 g a day**, five portions of 80 g. Potatoes don't count: they are a starchy food. A portion of legumes counts, but only once a day.

Frozen vegetables, canned tomatoes and sauerkraut count as much as fresh ones, and are cheaper out of season.

## Dietary patterns that work

The **Mediterranean diet** has the strongest evidence of any dietary pattern. In the PREDIMED trial (2013, republished with corrections in 2018), adding olive oil or nuts to it reduced heart attacks, strokes and cardiovascular deaths by about 30 % compared with a low-fat diet. The **DASH** diet lowers blood pressure about as much as a medication.

They share the same core:

- plenty of vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, nuts and seeds,
- unsaturated oils as the main fat,
- fish regularly, poultry and dairy in moderation,
- little red and processed meat, little sugar, little salt.

Traditional Romanian cooking already has many of these dishes: bean and lentil stews, vegetable soups (*ciorbe*), *zacuscă*, *ghiveci*, stuffed peppers, sauerkraut, fresh cheese and yogurt. The less healthy parts are mostly the **quantities** of cured meats, fried food, salt, white bread and sweets.

## Processed food

Not all processing is bad: frozen vegetables, canned fish, plain yogurt and wholemeal bread are processed. **Ultra-processed foods** are industrial products made largely from refined ingredients and additives: soft drinks, packaged snacks, sweets, instant noodles, many ready meals, reconstituted meat. Diets high in them are linked to weight gain and chronic disease, and in a controlled trial (Hall, 2019), people offered ultra-processed meals ate about 500 kcal a day more than when offered unprocessed meals matched for nutrients.

A useful rule: **most of what you eat should be food your grandparents would recognize as food.**
