# A world tour: pa, ba, da, ta, ka, la, si

This page continues the tour with syllables that start with a **stop** ([p b t d k g]), a liquid ([l]) or a fricative ([s ʃ]).

## pa and ba

```bash
node syllables.js --compare pa ba
```

```

PA (9 entries)
  Mandarin  怕            pà (tone 4)           to fear
            爬            pá (tone 2)           to climb, to crawl
  Bengali   পা           pa                    foot, leg
  Hindi     पा           pā-                   get, obtain (stem of pānā)  [stem]
  Romanian  pa           pa                    bye (informal)
  French    pas          pas                   step; not (in ne... pas)  (the s is silent: pronounced [pa])
  Spanish   papa / papá / Papa papa                  potato (Americas) / dad / the Pope
  Swahili   -pa          -pa                   give (ku-pa 'to give')  [stem]
            pa-          pa-                   prefix of class 16: a definite place  [prefix]

BA (7 entries)
  Mandarin    八            bā (tone 1)           eight
              拔            bá (tone 2)           to pull out
              把            bǎ (tone 3)           to hold; marker of a direct object
              爸            bà (tone 4)           dad
              吧            ba (neutral tone)     suggestion particle ('let's...', 'right?')  [particle]
  Vietnamese  ba           ba                    three; father (southern)
  Turkish     baba         baba                  father (ba-ba)
```

- **Father** again: Mandarin *bà*, Vietnamese *ba*, Turkish *baba*. Chapter 3 explains why stops like [p] and [b] go with fathers as nasals go with mothers.
- Spanish shows how much one syllable pair can carry: *papa* "potato" (in the Americas, from Quechua), *papá* "dad", and *Papa* "the Pope", distinguished by stress and context.
- Mandarin *bā* "eight" is why 8 is a lucky number in Chinese cultures: it sounds like *fā*, "to prosper" (in Cantonese, *baat* and *faat* are even closer). The Beijing Olympics opened on 8 August 2008 at 8:08 pm.
- Romanian *pa* is how friends say goodbye; Swahili *-pa* means "give".

## da

```bash
node syllables.js da
```

```
"da": 13 meanings in 10 languages

  Russian         да           da                    yes
  Romanian        da           da                    yes
  Serbo-Croatian  da           da                    yes; that (conjunction)
  Georgian        და           da                    and; sister
  German          da           da                    there; since, because
  Spanish         da           da                    (he/she) gives
  Mandarin        搭            dā (tone 1)           to put up; to take (a bus)
                  答            dá (tone 2)           to answer
                  打            dǎ (tone 3)           to hit; to do (many actions: dǎ diànhuà, to phone)
                  大            dà (tone 4)           big
  Japanese        だ            da                    is (plain copula)
  Turkish         -da / -de    -da                   in, at (locative suffix); also, too (as a separate word)  [suffix]
  Vietnamese      da           da                    skin; leather
```

- **Yes**: Russian, Romanian and Serbo-Croatian *da*. Romanian, a Romance language, borrowed it from its Slavic neighbors: Latin had no single word for *yes*.
- **Give**: Spanish *da* "gives" continues Latin *dat*, from the Indo-European root *\*deh₃-* "give", which also gives Russian *dat'* "to give", Sanskrit *dadāti* "gives", and Romanian *dă* "gives".
- Georgian *da* means both "and" and "sister": two homophones in one syllable.
- Mandarin *dǎ* "hit" is a busy verb: *dǎ diànhuà* "to make a phone call", *dǎ qiú* "to play ball", *dǎ zì* "to type".

## ta and to

```bash
node syllables.js --compare ta to
```

```

TA (6 entries)
  Mandarin  他 / 她 / 它    tā (tone 1)           he / she / it: three characters, one syllable
            塔            tǎ (tone 3)           tower, pagoda
  Japanese  田            ta                    rice field (Tanaka, 'in the middle of the fields')
            -た           -ta                   past tense ending (tabe-ta, ate)  [suffix]
  Swahili   -ta-         -ta-                  future tense marker (ni-ta-soma, I will read)  [particle]
  Korean    타            ta                    ride; burn (stem of tada)  [stem]

TO (5 entries)
  Japanese  と            to                    and, with; quotation marker  [particle]
            戸            to                    door
  Polish    to           to                    this, it
  Russian   то           to                    that (neuter)
  Hindi     तो           to                    then, so; as for (topic)  [particle]
```

- Mandarin *tā* is one of the neatest examples of writing distinguishing what speech doesn't: *he* 他, *she* 她 and *it* 它 are all pronounced *tā*. Spoken Mandarin doesn't mark gender in the third person at all; the written distinction was introduced in the early 20th century.
- Japanese *-ta* marks the past (*taberu* "eat", *tabeta* "ate"), and Swahili *-ta-* the future (*ninasoma* "I am reading", *nitasoma* "I will read"): opposite times, same syllable.

## ka and ga

```bash
node syllables.js --compare ka ga
```

```

KA (7 entries)
  Hindi     का           kā                    of, 's (possessive, before a masculine noun)  [particle]
  Japanese  か            ka                    question particle  [particle]
            蚊            ka                    mosquito
  Mandarin  卡            kǎ (tone 3)           card; (colloquial) to lag, to freeze
  Tagalog   ka           ka                    you (singular, after the verb)
  Estonian  ka           ka                    also, too
  Swahili   -ka-         -ka-                  verb marker: 'and then' (narrative sequence)  [particle]

GA (2 entries)
  Japanese  が            ga                    subject particle; but  [particle]
  Korean    가            ga                    subject particle (after a vowel); go (stem of gada)  [particle]
```

Japanese *ka* at the end of a sentence makes it a question (*Nihonjin desu ka?*, "Are you Japanese?"), and *ga* marks the subject. Korean *ga* is also a subject marker (after a vowel). Japanese and Korean are not known to be related, and the resemblance is usually considered a coincidence; this is exactly the kind of case where linguists ask for systematic evidence, not single look-alikes (chapter 4). Hindi *kā* means "of": *Rām kā ghar*, "Ram's house".

## la

```bash
node syllables.js la
```

```
"la": 11 meanings in 9 languages

  Spanish     la           la                    the (feminine); her, it (object)
  French      la / là      la                    the (feminine) / there
  Italian     la / là      la                    the (feminine) / there
  Romanian    la           la                    at, to
  Arabic      لا           lā                    no, not
  Mandarin    拉            lā (tone 1)           to pull
              辣            là (tone 4)           spicy hot
  Vietnamese  là           là (falling)          is, to be (copula)
              lá           lá (rising)           leaf
  Hindi       ला           lā-                   bring (stem of lānā)  [stem]
  Swahili     -la          -la                   eat (ku-la 'to eat')  [stem]
```

- Spanish, French and Italian *la* "the (feminine)" all come from Latin *illa* "that (woman)": the Latin demonstrative wore down into the article. Romanian took a different path: its articles come from the same Latin word but are attached at the *end* of the noun: *casa* "the house", from *casă* + *-a*.
- Arabic *lā* "no": one of the first words learners meet.
- Vietnamese *là* "is" and *lá* "leaf" differ by tone only.

## si and shi

```bash
node syllables.js --compare si shi
```

```

SI (13 entries)
  Spanish    si / sí      si                    if / yes
  Italian    sì / si      si                    yes / oneself (reflexive)
  French     si           si                    if; so; yes (contradicting a negative question)
  Mandarin   丝            sī (tone 1)           silk; thread
             死            sǐ (tone 3)           to die
             四            sì (tone 4)           four
  Cantonese  詩            si1                   poem
             史            si2                   history
             試            si3                   to try
             時            si4                   time
             市            si5                   market; city
             事            si6                   matter, affair
  Swahili    si           si                    am not, is not (negative copula); I do not (verb prefix)

SHI (6 entries)
  Mandarin  诗            shī (tone 1)          poem
            十            shí (tone 2)          ten
            史            shǐ (tone 3)          history
            是            shì (tone 4)          to be; yes
  Japanese  四 / 死        shi                   four / death (so 'four' is often read yon)
  Romanian  și           și                    and; also
```

- **Yes and if**: Spanish *sí* "yes" and *si* "if" are told apart only by an accent in writing. Both come from Latin: *sic* "thus, so" gave the *yes*, and *si* "if" stayed *si*. French *si* answers a negative question: *Tu ne viens pas? Si!*, "You're not coming? Yes, I am!"
- **Four and death**: in Mandarin, *sì* "four" and *sǐ* "die" differ only in tone; in Japanese and Korean, the Chinese-derived words for "four" and "death" are identical (*shi*, *sa*). As a result, many buildings in East Asia skip the 4th floor, and Japanese often uses the native word *yon* for "four" instead of *shi*.
- **Cantonese** uses all six of its tones on *si*: poem, history, try, time, market, matter.
