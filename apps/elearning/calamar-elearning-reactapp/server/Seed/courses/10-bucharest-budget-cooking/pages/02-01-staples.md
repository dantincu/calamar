## The cheapest nutrition

With these prices, `node unit-price.js --rank` ranks the pantry by what each leu buys:

- **Protein**, for 20 g: white beans 0.77 lei, lentils 0.85, chickpeas 1.17, oats 1.21, chicken legs 2.35, eggs 2.53, chicken breast 2.67, sardines 3.25, mackerel 3.58.
- **Fiber**, for 10 g: white beans 0.59 lei, oats 0.79, lentils and chickpeas 0.98, cabbage 1.18.
- **Energy**, for 1000 kcal: flour 0.96 lei, sunflower oil 1.02, sugar 1.16, cornmeal 1.35, oats 2.11.

The energy list is a warning as well as a guide: oil and sugar are the cheapest calories, which is one reason cheap diets so often become unhealthy ones. Spend the budget on legumes, vegetables, oats, eggs and fish first, then add energy with cornmeal, potatoes, bread and oil in measured amounts.

## What to skip

These cost a lot for what they give:

- **Ready meals, packaged snacks, soft drinks and sweets**: expensive per kilogram, and the main source of salt, sugar and fat.
- **Cured meats** (salami, *parizer*, sausages): 30–60 lei/kg, often more than half fat, and salty.
- **Bottled water**: Bucharest's tap water is safe to drink; a jug filter costs less than a month of bottled water.
- **"Superfoods"**: chia, quinoa, goji, at 40–100 lei/kg. Oats, beans, cabbage, sunflower seeds and walnuts give similar nutrients for a fraction of the price.
- **Pre-cut vegetables and salad bags**: you pay two to four times the price per kilogram for the knife work.
